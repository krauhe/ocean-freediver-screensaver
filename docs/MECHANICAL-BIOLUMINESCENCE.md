# Mechanical bioluminescence coverage

`marine-wakes.js` connects the recreational freediver's skinned fin edges and moving hands, turtle foreflippers, manta wing edges and escaping school fish to the existing shared bioluminescence pool. Edge motion is measured relative to local current. The manta samples use the same wing-deformation formula as its vertex shader; turtle distal shader flex is approximated by the articulated pivot. Fin/hand samples follow the recreational skeleton and blade spring.

**Evidence:** [Latz Laboratory, Dinoflagellate Bioluminescence](https://latzlab.ucsd.edu/bioluminescence/dinoflagellates/dinoflagellate-bioluminescence/) explains that sufficient mechanical stress from swimming animals, humans and waves can stimulate flashes. Indexed primary-laboratory text reviewed 2026-11-02. **Simplified implementation:** speed/activity thresholds stand in for local fluid shear and plankton sensitivity; this is not a turbulence solver or emission by the animals themselves.

Existing shark, competition-diver, scuba, bubble and boat wake emitters remain. Jellyfish/comb-jelly body-light displays are separate effects. Slow crab/bottom-life motion does not receive a new constant halo; sediment effects remain separate. Ordinary calm schooling also does not receive a constant glow: the new fish contribution requires an active escape response and sufficient speed. At most 24 fish are sampled per rendered frame, rotating through individuals. All emitters use the existing amount slider and shared particle budget. Daylight, pauses, hidden models, large time jumps and reappearances suppress inappropriate bursts.

Local verification: `validation/marine-wakes-check.cjs` runs two minutes with night light and a controlled fish-escape state; all four source categories submit emissions, pause and daylight suppress them, and no runtime/shader errors occur. `validation/recreational-arms-check.cjs` verifies ten minutes of exploration, ascent, bounded hand motion and pause after increasing ascent speed and kick sweep. Ignored validation artifacts are local, not bundled assets.

Recreational ascent now requests 1.2 scene metres/s below 3 m surface depth, tapering to 0.75 nearer the surface. Actual speed still follows bounded propulsion, water resistance and depth-dependent buoyancy. These are animation targets; leg sweep is strengthened with the same effort signal. There is no prescribed apnea/ascent protocol.
